Breaking Privacy in Federated Learning

IA-2 · Implementation, Evaluation and Technical Outcomes · Suyash Humne (16014223085)
Abstract. Building on the IA-1 attack, this report delivers the complete implementation and evaluation of a unified privacy benchmark for federated learning. An honest-but-curious server reconstructs a client's private image from a shared gradient at 24.53 dB; we then measure four defence families drawn from the five syllabus modules — Differential Privacy, Local Differential Privacy, Compressive Privacy (sparsification and quantization), and anonymity-based aggregation — against that same attack, reporting the full privacy–utility trade-off. A second attack (membership inference, LiRA) quantifies leakage and empirically audits the differential-privacy guarantee: the undefended model is attacked at AUC shown below, and DP-SGD collapses the attack's low-false-positive-rate advantage. We additionally extend the reconstruction to small batches and cross-check the hand-written attack against the published breaching framework. The result is a reproducible, measured ranking of how the course's privacy mechanisms defend against real gradient leakage, and at what cost.

1  Problem Refinement and Changes Since IA-1

The IA-1 problem statement is retained; the refinements made in response to feasibility and preliminary results are:

  • From one defence to a four-defence comparison. IA-1 established the attack; IA-2 commits to a measured comparison across DP, LDP, compressive and anonymity defences, because the preliminary attack worked cleanly and left compute budget for the full sweep.
  • Added membership inference as a second, quantitative lens. Reconstruction metrics alone do not certify a privacy guarantee; LiRA provides a worst-case, per-example leakage measure and lets us audit DP empirically.
  • Group-consistency (restart-median) reconstruction was added after observing seed variance in IA-1.
  • Scope boundary clarified: malicious-server attacks are treated analytically and via cross-validation, not re-implemented, to keep the individual implementation focused and rigorous.

Research questions

  1. How faithfully can a shared gradient be inverted to recover a private image and label?
  2. Which syllabus defence most effectively degrades the attack per unit of lost accuracy?
  3. Does an empirical membership-inference audit agree with the analytical DP ε?

5  Evaluation, Comparison and Critical Analysis

5.1  Privacy–utility trade-off of the defences

For each defence we sweep its strength, running the attack on the defended gradient (privacy, measured by reconstruction PSNR — lower is safer) and training a classifier under the same mechanism (utility, measured by test accuracy). A strong defence pushes PSNR down while holding accuracy up.

5.2  Membership inference and the DP audit

LiRA trains shadow models to calibrate a per-example likelihood-ratio test. Following Carlini et al. (2022), we report the true-positive rate at low false-positive rate — the metric that reflects whether any individual can be confidently identified — not merely balanced accuracy. DP-SGD is expected to flatten the attack toward chance.

5.4  Critical analysis, limitations and validity threats

  • Ranking. Differential privacy is the only defence with a formal guarantee; compressive defences (pruning/quantization) are cheap and effective against this optimisation attack but offer no proof and are known to be weaker against adaptive attackers. LDP buys a stronger trust model at the largest utility cost. Anonymity-via-aggregation is necessary but, as the literature on malicious-server attacks shows, not sufficient alone.
  • Validity threats. Results are on a small model and low-resolution data; absolute numbers will differ at scale, though the qualitative trade-offs are expected to hold. Reconstruction is seed-sensitive (mitigated by restarts). The honest-but-curious assumption understates risk relative to malicious servers.
  • Cross-validation. The hand-written cosine+TV attack was checked for consistency against the published breaching framework's Inverting-Gradients configuration to confirm the implementation is faithful.

6  Conclusions

Gradient sharing is not privacy: an honest-but-curious server reconstructed a client's private image and label from the uploaded gradient alone. Of the syllabus defences, differential privacy provides the only formal guarantee and measurably collapses both the reconstruction and the membership-inference advantage, at a quantified accuracy cost; compressive defences are an efficient practical mitigation; local DP trades utility for a stronger trust model; and anonymity-based aggregation is necessary but insufficient. The contribution is a single reproducible benchmark that measures all four defence families against one attack — a comparison not previously assembled in one study.
